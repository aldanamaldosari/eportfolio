The task

Run four programmes (covariance and Pearson correlation, linear regression, multiple linear regression and polynomial regression) and change the variables to see how the data points affect correlation and regression.

Each notebook was run as given and then extended with experiments: more or less noise, fewer points, outliers, removed points, different units and higher polynomial degrees. The lab below runs the same calculations in the browser, so every change can be tried directly on this page.

What changed

        
Results from the executed notebooks, which use the notebook data and seeds:

        

          
Correlation
            
Noise alone took r from 1.00 (no noise) to 0.89 (notebook) to 0.21 (noise sd 100).
              With 5 points, r ranged from −0.30 to 1.00 over 200 samples. With 1000 points it
              stayed between 0.88 and 0.91. Multiplying data1 by 1000 multiplied the covariance
              by 1000 and left r at 0.888. A U-shaped relationship scored r ≈ 0.01. One outlier
              in a 20-point sample turned r from 0.89 to −0.28.

          
Linear regression
            
The notebook fit has r = −0.76 (R² 0.58). Removing (17, 86) gave r = −0.93 and a
              steeper slope (−2.88). Removing the two x = 2 points flattened the slope to −1.02.
              Adding one outlier at (20, 120) flattened the slope to −0.05 and r to −0.02.

          
Multiple regression
            
Weight and Volume together explain only 38% of the variation in CO2, barely more
              than Volume alone (35%), because the two inputs are correlated (r = 0.75). Dropping
              the four cars over 1600 kg made the Weight coefficient negative. The notebook's
              2300 kg prediction is heavier than any car in the data.

          
Polynomial regression
            
Training R² rose from 0.18 (degree 1) to 0.94 (degree 3) to 0.99 (degree 9). The
              leave-one-out error was lowest at degree 3 (4.2) and highest at degree 9 (20.6).
              At hour 24, outside the data, degrees 3 to 9 predicted anywhere from 33 to 181.
