Intelligent Agents

This module explores agent-based computing, from the main agent architectures and their roots in AI research to applying them to real-world problems under uncertainty. It covers designing, building and evaluating an agent-based system with legal, ethical and professional issues in mind, and working effectively in a virtual development team.

Intelligent agents are software programs that perceive their environment and act within it to pursue a goal. The module covers both the individual agent and the interactions between agents, moving from the motivations for agent-based computing through the main agent models in use today and their grounding in artificial intelligence research.

The work is practical as well as theoretical: designing, deploying and evaluating agent systems in languages such as Java and Python, drawing on skills built in earlier modules, and reading into current research issues including state-of-the-art models and recent developments in deployment.

Module aims

  • Understand the motivations for, and appropriate use of, agent-based computing.
  • Understand the main agent models in use today and their grounding in AI research.
  • Develop, deploy and evaluate intelligent systems to solve real-world problems.
  • Understand contemporary research issues in intelligent agent systems.

Team design proposal

Unit 6 · Group E

Completed Submitted 7 September 2026

Group E is building an LLM-powered planning agent for academic research: it takes a topic, plans its own search, retrieves and validates sources, and exports a structured result. My role is Methodology Lead, contributing the approach and methodology behind the six-agent architecture.

Submission

  • Team report — the group design proposal
  • Individual peer review evaluation

What I contributed

  • Selected Prometheus over Gaia as the agent-oriented design methodology, and set it alongside Scrum for team delivery.
  • Derived the six-agent architecture — Orchestrator, Planner, Academic Retrieval, Processing and Ranking, Evidence Validator, Storage and Export.
  • Drew the boundary between agents and capabilities, keeping frameworks such as LangGraph out of the agent set.
  • Defined the two-level planning approach: Plan-and-Execute for the outer loop, ReAct within subtasks.
  • Specified re-planning through the Orchestrator rather than local retry when evidence is judged insufficient.
  • Set retrieval as a structured-API RAG pipeline, and bounded autonomy to a single human approval before export.
  • Wrote the Approach and Methodology section, its references and the terminology appendix.
