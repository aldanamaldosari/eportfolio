The task

Identify a deployed application of deep learning, explain how it works, and discuss its impacts.

Live facial recognition for policing

The live facial recognition (LFR) scans every person passing by a camera and compares it to a police watchlist in real time. The match alerts an officer who decides whether to stop the person. Police forces are using this tool now in dense public places — a real-time interaction between deep learning and everyday people.

How it works

LFR uses convolutional neural networks, which already outperform humans in large-scale image recognition (Thomsen, 2015; Or, 2025). A face is detected and sent through a deep network to an embedding vector. The training process layers images of the same person close together with different people far apart (Schroff, Kalenichenko and Philbin, 2015). Each live embedding is compared with the watchlist, and if it has a similarity score above a set threshold, then the alert is raised. That threshold is the key setting: lowering it results in more suspects but also more false alerts.

Impacts

The benefits are real: finding suspects of serious crime and finding missing people. The costs are also concrete. Everyone in the vicinity is biometrically processed, not just suspects, which undermines anonymity in public space. Accuracy is also uneven: commercial face-analysis systems have been shown to have much higher error rates for darker-skinned women than lighter-skinned men (Buolamwini and Gebru, 2018). Such errors are wrongful stops for some groups. Radanliev et al. (2024) argue that responsible deployment is dependent on governance over the whole lifecycle, not just model accuracy.

The agent view

From the agent's perspective, LFR is a perceive–deliberate–act system (Russell and Norvig, 2022). The network perceives and, in turn, the officer should think through it. And when officers simply rely on alerts, that deliberation evaporates. Safeguards should include published thresholds, independent bias testing at those thresholds, narrow watchlists, and treating an alert as a lead, not as an identification.
